How laser tattoo removal works

Laser tattoo removal is a gradual process. The laser does not simply erase ink in one appointment; treatment begins a biological clearance process that continues for weeks after each session.

Targeting tattoo pigment

Controlled pulses of laser energy are directed into the tattoo. Appropriate wavelengths are selected to target particular pigment characteristics while the surrounding skin is monitored carefully. The aim is to break larger pigment deposits into smaller particles.

Gradual pigment clearance

After treatment, the body gradually processes fragmented pigment. This is why a tattoo may continue to fade between appointments and why closely repeated sessions do not necessarily create faster or safer progress.

Repeated, measured sessions

Most tattoos need a series of treatments. Settings, intervals and expectations are reviewed as the tattoo responds. Progress may be uneven because different areas can contain different colours, depths and densities of ink.

Q-Switched Nd:YAG laser technology

BARE by Marlese uses Q-Switched Nd:YAG laser technology for tattoo-removal treatments. Q-Switched systems deliver very short pulses designed to target pigment. The 1064 nm and 532 nm wavelengths are commonly associated with different pigment responses and are selected according to the tattoo and suitability assessment.

Technology alone does not determine the outcome. Practitioner assessment, correct settings, treatment spacing, skin response, aftercare and realistic expectations all matter. A powerful device used without appropriate planning is not a substitute for a careful treatment pathway.

Why tattoo colours respond differently

Black and dark inks often absorb commonly used tattoo-removal wavelengths more predictably. Some red or warmer pigments may respond to another wavelength, while certain blues, greens, pale colours and mixed pigments can be more resistant. Cosmetic pigments also require special caution because some can change colour when exposed to laser energy.

A multicoloured tattoo may therefore fade at different speeds. It is normal for one section to show visible change before another, and no responsible clinic should promise identical results for every colour or every client.

What affects the number of sessions?

Online estimates can only be general. A tattoo's response depends on several interacting factors, and even two visually similar tattoos may not fade at the same rate.

Ink colour and density

Dense black linework, shading, layered colour and heavily saturated sections can require different treatment approaches. Some pigments fragment readily, while others are slower or less predictable.

Professional or amateur tattoo

Professional tattoos often contain more consistent and deeper pigment, while amateur tattoos may be shallower but uneven. Neither type guarantees an easy or difficult removal journey; the individual tattoo still needs assessment.

Age and placement

Older tattoos may already have faded naturally. Placement also matters because circulation, tissue characteristics and distance from the centre of the body may influence how efficiently fragmented pigment is cleared.

Skin and healing response

Skin type, sensitivity, previous scarring, general health and healing history are considered. Treatment must remain safe for the skin rather than chasing faster fading at any cost.

Layering and cover-ups

A tattoo placed over an older design can contain multiple layers of pigment. These layers may reveal themselves gradually as the upper design fades, extending the treatment journey.

Your treatment goal

Fading for a cover-up may require fewer sessions than pursuing maximum possible removal. Progress is reviewed against your actual objective rather than an arbitrary session number.

Who may be suitable?

Laser tattoo removal may be considered by adults who want to reduce the appearance of an unwanted tattoo or prepare it for a new design. Suitable clients understand that treatment is gradual, results vary and multiple sessions are usually required. They are also willing to follow aftercare and allow appropriate healing time between appointments.

Suitability is based on the tattoo, skin, medical history, medication, healing, sun exposure and treatment expectations. It is not decided from tattoo size alone.

When treatment may need to wait

Treatment may be postponed when the area is sunburnt, recently tanned, infected, irritated, broken or not fully healed. Certain medications, photosensitivity, recent procedures, pregnancy, breastfeeding or relevant health conditions may also affect timing or suitability.

Always disclose health changes, medication and recent sun exposure before treatment. Do not stop prescribed medication unless instructed by the professional responsible for your care.

What happens during a session?

Pre-treatment check

The area is reviewed for healing, irritation, tanning and any changes since booking. Medication, health changes and recent exposure should be disclosed before proceeding.

Preparation and protection

The tattoo area is prepared and appropriate protective measures are used. Photographs may be taken with consent so progress can be compared under similar conditions.

Laser pulses

Controlled pulses are delivered across the planned treatment area. Clients often describe a hot, sharp snapping sensation. Treatment time depends on tattoo size and complexity.

Immediate skin response

The treated area is checked for the expected response. Temporary whitening or frosting may appear, along with redness, warmth or swelling. These reactions are assessed and documented.

Cooling and protection

Cooling or protective measures are applied according to the skin response and treatment protocol. The goal is to support comfort without interfering with normal healing.

Aftercare and next steps

You receive guidance on cleaning, protection, activity, sun avoidance and signs that should be reported. The next session is spaced to allow adequate healing and pigment clearance.

What does frosting mean?

Frosting is the temporary pale or white appearance sometimes seen immediately after laser pulses. It can look dramatic, but it is usually short-lived. Frosting is caused by a rapid surface reaction associated with the laser interacting with pigment and moisture in the skin.

Frosting does not show the final amount of fading and should not be used as a guarantee of outcome. The meaningful change develops over the following weeks as the area heals and fragmented pigment is gradually processed.

Important: Do not rub, scratch or pick the treated area to remove visible frosting, crusting or dryness. Follow the aftercare advice provided.

Expected progress and realistic results

Immediately after treatment, the tattoo may look lighter because of frosting or surrounding redness. This is not the final result. Over the following days and weeks, the skin settles and gradual pigment change may become easier to assess.

Some tattoos show noticeable fading after early sessions, while others change slowly. Certain colours, dense sections or layered areas may remain visible even after a long treatment course. Significant fading may be achievable, but complete removal cannot be guaranteed.

Laser tattoo removal aftercare

Correct aftercare protects healing skin and reduces avoidable irritation. Follow the personalised instructions supplied after every session, even when a previous treatment healed easily.

Keep the area clean and protected

Follow the cleaning and dressing guidance provided. Use clean hands, avoid unnecessary touching and do not apply unapproved products. Allow the skin to settle naturally.

Avoid picking and friction

Do not scratch, pick, shave over, scrub or deliberately remove dry skin or crusting. Avoid tight clothing, repetitive rubbing and activities that repeatedly irritate the treated area.

Avoid heat, swimming and sun

Avoid excessive heat, saunas, steam rooms and swimming during the advised initial period. Protect the area from sunlight and do not expose recently treated skin to tanning beds.

Laser Tattoo Removal Didcot FAQ

Can a tattoo be completely removed?

Some tattoos can fade very significantly, but complete removal cannot be promised. Ink colour, depth, density, tattoo age, placement, layering and individual clearance all affect the final result.

How many sessions will I need?

Most tattoos need multiple sessions. A reliable exact number cannot be given before seeing how the tattoo responds. Cover-up fading may need fewer sessions than pursuing maximum possible removal.

Does tattoo removal hurt?

Clients commonly describe a hot snapping sensation. Comfort depends on location, size and sensitivity. Treatment is usually brief for small tattoos, and suitable cooling measures are used.

Can you fade my tattoo for a cover-up?

Yes. Partial fading can reduce the density of the existing design and give your tattoo artist more options. Your artist may advise how much fading they want before beginning a new design.

What is frosting?

Frosting is temporary whitening that may appear immediately after the laser pulse. It is an expected short-term response and does not represent the final amount of fading.

How long should I wait between sessions?

Sessions are spaced several weeks apart to allow healing and pigment clearance. Your interval may change according to skin response, tattoo location and progress.

Are black tattoos easiest to remove?

Black and darker inks often respond more predictably to commonly used tattoo-removal wavelengths. This does not guarantee complete removal, and density, depth and layering still matter.

What about red, blue or green ink?

Different colours absorb different wavelengths and can fade at different rates. Some pigments are more resistant, and a multicoloured tattoo may change unevenly across a course.

Will laser tattoo removal scar?

Any skin treatment carries risk. Existing tattoo scarring may become more visible as pigment fades. Correct settings, suitable spacing and aftercare are important, but no clinic can promise zero risk.

Can I exercise after treatment?

You may be advised to avoid intense exercise, excessive heat, friction or activities that irritate the area during the initial healing period. Follow the instructions provided for your treatment site.

Do I need a consultation or patch test?

Tattoo clients can begin through the online booking pathway. A consultation, further assessment or patch test may still be recommended where clinically appropriate for the tattoo, skin or medical history.
